# Figures we do not target

A redraw replaces a scan only when it can replace it COMPLETELY. Where the figure IS
the illustration -- a shaded 3-D fan, a person, a freeform surface -- a partial redraw
is worse than the scan: it looks authoritative and is missing the thing the figure is
about. These keep their scans.

The test is not "does it contain a picture" but "is there a diagram left once the
picture is removed". Measured: each of these left 1-12 primitives, against 46-85 for
figures where the pictorial element is incidental decoration and the physics survives.

- **PHY11L_CH06_P12** — a ceiling fan and a potter's wheel, both shaded 3-D solids
- **PHY11L_CH06_P16** — an oscillating table fan: shaded blades, guard ring, conical stand
- **PHY11L_CH06_P272** — a freehand pen sketch of a girl on a swivel chair
- **PHY12L_CH01_P129** — half the figure is an arbitrary closed surface drawn as a freeform blob
- **PHY12L_CH03_P01** — nothing drawable survived -- 1 primitive

## Added after a second look (a count threshold was the wrong test)

`PHY11L_CH04_P41` reached 26 primitives and still had to go. The count said it was
mostly geometry; what NCERT prints is a girl swinging a stone, and the caption says
"we can FEEL this" -- the demonstration IS the figure. The test is therefore
CATEGORICAL, not numeric: a person or a rendered real object as the SUBJECT puts a
figure out of scope however much geometry surrounds it.

- **PHY11L_CH04_P41** — a girl swinging a stone on a string -- the demonstration IS the figure
- **PHY11L_CH04_P91** — a hand holding a rod stirring water, and a hand gripping a glass
