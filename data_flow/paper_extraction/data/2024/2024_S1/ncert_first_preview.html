NCERT BIO11L-CH09 · 9.1 How to Analyse Chemical Composition? · Botany
Lecithin, a small molecular weight organic compound found in living tissues, is an example of:
  • Carbohydrates
  • Amino acids
  • Phospholipids
  • Glycerides

Solution

What NCERT saysSome lipids have phosphorous and a phosphorylated organic compound in them. These are phospholipids. They are found in cell membrane. Lecithin is one example.BIO11L-CH09-P09

Lecithin is the example NCERT itself gives. In the section on lipids the book says that some lipids have phosphorous and a phosphorylated organic compound in them, that these are phospholipids, that they are found in cell membrane, and that lecithin is one example. So lecithin belongs with the phospholipids. The book also keeps phospholipids apart from the plain glycerides. Fats and oils are glycerides, in which fatty acids are esterified to glycerol, and these are the monoglycerides, diglycerides and triglycerides. Phospholipids contain, in addition, a phosphorylated nitrogenous compound. That added phosphorylated part is what puts lecithin with the phospholipids and not simply with the glycerides. The stem's small molecular weight organic compound is NCERT's own way of describing these biomolecules.

  • (1) NCERT counts monosaccharide and disaccharide sugars as their own group of small molecular weight organic compounds. Lecithin is not named among the sugars; the book names it in the lipids section.
  • (2) Amino acids are a separate group in NCERT's list of organic compounds, and the book says there are 20 types of them. Lecithin is not one of them; it is named as an example of a phospholipid.
  • (4) In NCERT, glycerides are the fats and oils, the monoglycerides, diglycerides and triglycerides, in which fatty acids are esterified to glycerol. Phospholipids contain, in addition, a phosphorylated nitrogenous compound, and lecithin is the example the book gives of a phospholipid, not of a glyceride.

NCERT BIO11L-CH06 · 6.2.6 Isobilateral (Monocotyledonous) Leaf · Botany
Bulliform cells are responsible for
  • Providing large spaces for storage of sugars.
  • Inward curling of leaves in monocots.
  • Protecting the plant from salt stress.
  • Increased photosynthesis in monocots.

Solution

What NCERT saysWhen they are flaccid due to water stress, they make the leaves curl inwards to minimise water loss.BIO11L-CH06-P19

Bulliform cells sit on the upper side of a grass leaf. NCERT says certain adaxial epidermal cells along the veins modify themselves into large, empty, colourless cells, and calls these bulliform cells. What they do depends on water. When the bulliform cells in the leaves have absorbed water and are turgid, the leaf surface is exposed. When they are flaccid due to water stress, they make the leaves curl inwards to minimise water loss. That inward curling is what the question is asking for, so option 2 is correct. The grass leaf is the isobilateral (monocotyledonous) leaf of this section, which is why the option says monocots. The book describes these cells only as large, empty and colourless, and gives them no other job.

  • (1) NCERT calls bulliform cells large, empty and colourless, and gives them no storage job at all. The word empty is what makes this option look right, but in the book emptiness is only how the cells are described, not something they hold.
  • (3) The only stress NCERT names here is water stress: the cells go flaccid due to water stress and curl the leaf to minimise water loss. The passage says nothing about salt.
  • (4) NCERT gives bulliform cells no such role; it calls them colourless. It says a turgid state leaves the leaf surface exposed and a flaccid state curls the leaf inwards, so their action hides leaf surface rather than adding to it.

  • $11^{\mathrm{th}}$ segment

Solution

What NCERT saysThe latest NCERT edition does not cover this. This chapter no longer carries cockroach anatomy, so there is no line in the current book that settles where the anal cerci sit.

The question hands you the structures themselves: a pair of jointed filamentous anal cerci, present in the male and the female alike. All it asks is where on the animal they sit. They are terminal appendages, projecting from the hind end of the abdomen, on its last segment, which is the tenth. So the answer is option 3. Because they occur in both sexes, they cannot be one of the parts that distinguish a male cockroach from a female; those lie just ahead of them. Be aware that the latest NCERT Biology does not teach cockroach anatomy at all, as this chapter now deals only with the frog, so there is no line in the current book to revise this fact from.

  • (1) The adult abdomen ends at the tenth segment; there is no eleventh segment left bearing appendages, so nothing can be carried there.
  • (2) The fifth segment lies in the middle of the abdomen. The cerci are a hind-end pair, borne on the last segment, not halfway along the body.
  • (4) The eighth and ninth segments lie ahead of the terminal one and are associated with the external genital structures, which differ between male and female. The stem states the cerci are present in both sexes, so they are not borne there.

NCERT BIO11L-CH17 · 17.2 Muscle · Zoology
Three types of muscles are given as a, b and c. Identify the correct matching pair along with their location in human body: Name of muscle/location
  • (a) Involuntary – Nose tip (b) Skeletal – Bone (c) Cardiac – Heart
  • (a) Smooth - Toes (b) Skeletal – Legs (c) Cardiac – Heart
  • (a) Skeletal - Triceps (b) Smooth – Stomach (c) Smooth – Heart
  • (a) Skeletal - Biceps (b) Involuntary – Intestine (c) Cardiac – Heart

Solution

What NCERT saysAs the name suggests, Cardiac muscles are the muscles of heart.BIO11L-CH17-P10

NCERT classifies muscles by location into three types: Skeletal, Visceral and Cardiac. Skeletal muscles are closely associated with the skeletal components of the body, so the biceps of the arm is a skeletal muscle. Visceral muscles are located in the inner walls of hollow visceral organs of the body like the alimentary canal, of which the intestine is a part. They do not exhibit any striation and are smooth in appearance, and their activities are not under the voluntary control of the nervous system, so the book also calls them involuntary muscles. The heart is the third case: cardiac muscles are the muscles of heart, and based on appearance they are striated. Only option 4 gets all three pairs right.

  • (1) (a) Involuntary - Nose tip does not match. NCERT places the involuntary (visceral) muscles in the inner walls of hollow visceral organs of the body like the alimentary canal and the reproductive tract, not at the nose tip. One wrong pair makes the whole option wrong, even though (c) Cardiac - Heart is right.
  • (2) (a) Smooth - Toes does not match. Smooth muscles are the visceral muscles of the inner walls of hollow visceral organs; the toes carry skeletal components, and muscles associated with skeletal components are skeletal muscles. (b) and (c) are right, but (a) fails.
  • (3) (c) Smooth - Heart does not match. NCERT states that cardiac muscles are the muscles of heart, and that based on appearance cardiac muscles are striated, whereas it is the smooth (nonstriated) muscles that do not exhibit any striation. (a) Skeletal - Triceps and (b) Smooth - Stomach are acceptable, but the heart pair is wrong.
