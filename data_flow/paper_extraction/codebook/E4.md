## 1. THE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST: Chemistry is never one subject here

**Condition any expectation about a Chemistry question on B7's `branch` field (physical / organic / inorganic). Never on the word "Chemistry".**

This held on **10 of 10 real papers, with no exceptions**:

| Branch | Behaves like | Dominant thinking |
|---|---|---|
| `physical` (kinetics, equilibrium, electrochemistry, solutions, thermodynamics, atomic structure) | **Physics** | `calculation`, `application` |
| `organic` | **Biology** | `recall`, `elimination` |
| `inorganic` | **Biology** | `recall`, `elimination` |

The split inside Chemistry runs roughly 40–60% on each side and varies by paper, not by year. Treating "Chemistry" as one bucket therefore mis-tags a large share of Chemistry questions in **both** directions: physical-chemistry items get tagged `recall` when they are calculations, and organic/inorganic items get tagged `calculation` or `application` when nothing is computed at all.

**What to do when `branch` is `unsure` or absent:** take no prior at all. Read the question cold. Do not substitute a Chemistry-wide default — that is the exact error this rule exists to prevent. Say in `why` that the branch was unavailable.

**What to do when `branch` disagrees with the question in front of you:** trust the question, not the field. B7's branch detection has a known keyword-fallback path and is not infallible. Note the disagreement in `why`.

### The subject priors in full — and what a prior is for

| Subject / branch | Confirmed real pattern |
|---|---|
| Physics | Numerical/application-dominated in **every** year sampled. In two of the ten papers, 100% of sampled Physics questions were numerical or application with **zero** pure recall. |
| Chemistry · physical | As Physics. |
| Chemistry · organic, inorganic | As Biology. |
| Biology | Recall/elimination-dominated in every year. `calculation` is **rare but real** — confirmed counter-examples: a PCR-amplification question needing 2ⁿ, and a dihybrid-cross phenotypic-ratio question. Never hard-exclude `calculation` for Biology. |

**A prior is only a sanity check on a tag you already decided by reading.** It is never evidence for a tag. If your tag contradicts the prior, that is normal — say why in one clause and keep your tag. If you find yourself reaching for the prior *before* you have read the options, stop and read them.

### The second rule: `thinking` can never be shortcut from `question_type`, `format` or subject

Confirmed in both directions on real 2024 data:

- A Physics **Assertion–Reason** question required a real calculation inside the A/R judgement. Assertion–Reason does **not** imply `explanatory_reasoning`.
- A Biology question that *looked* numerical ("how many molecules of ATP and NADPH …") was pure memorised recall with **zero** calculation. Number-shaped options do **not** imply `calculation`.

So: E1 and E2 are inputs to your reading, never substitutes for it. Every question gets a fresh per-question judgement.

## 2. How to decide, in order

1. Solve the question in your head, briefly. Notice where you could have gone wrong.
2. Ask: **what is the hard part?** Not "what topic is this", not "what shape is this". The step that a prepared student most plausibly fails is the thinking tag.
3. Use the D4 evidence to settle recall vs application: if an NCERT line **states** this answer (`link_type: states_answer`), the demand is recall-family. If NCERT states the idea and the question moves it to a case NCERT does not describe (`concept_applied`), the demand is `application`.
4. Pick the **primary**. Add a **secondary** only if a second, genuinely different kind of thinking is also required to get the answer — never to hedge.
5. Write `why` as one sentence a reviewer can check in seconds, naming the specific hard step. "Must judge four statements" is checkable. "Requires conceptual understanding" is not.

**Tie-break (SPEC 13/E4):** primary = whatever makes the question hard. At most one secondary.

## 3. The eight categories

### `recall`
- **Definition:** one remembered fact decides the answer. Once you know the fact, there is nothing left to do.
- **Tag when:** a single NCERT fact, term, number, name or classification settles it outright.
- **Don't tag when (vs `elimination`):** count the independent facts the student must settle. One fact → `recall`. Two or more separately-judged items → `elimination`. "How many plants among the following are monocots?" asks four separate recall judgements, so it is `elimination` even though each judgement alone is recall.
- **Don't tag when (vs `concept_discrimination`):** `recall` is "do you know it". `concept_discrimination` is "do you know it *and* the question has deliberately parked a look-alike next to it".
- **Don't tag when (vs `application`):** if the exact case is not in NCERT and a rule has to be carried to it, it is `application`.
- **Real example:** `Q-000011` — "errors due to unpredictable fluctuations in temperature and voltage supply are:" → random errors. One NCERT definition decides it. Options are four named error types, none a trap on the others.
- **Real example (the inverse trap):** the 2024 Biology "how many molecules of ATP and NADPH" item is `recall`, not `calculation` — the numbers are memorised, not computed.

### `elimination`
- **Definition:** several separate claims must each be judged true or false.
- **Tag when:** lettered statements A–E, `statement_i_ii`, four option-statements, or four candidate reactions/pairs where each must be evaluated independently and the answer is whichever survives.
- **Don't tag when (vs `recall`):** only one of the items is actually in doubt and the rest are obviously wrong. Then the real demand is the one fact → `recall`.
- **Don't tag when (vs `process`):** `elimination` judges items that are independent of each other. `process` judges one chain whose **order** is the difficulty.
- **Real example:** `Q-000015` — four statements (A oestrus cycle, B menopause, C pregnancy, D menarche) about the female reproductive cycle, options are combinations. Each statement needs its own verdict; the answer (A, C and D) is whatever is left after B is rejected. Primary `elimination`.
- **Real example (organic Chemistry):** `Q-000005` — "Which of the following reactions will NOT give primary amine as the product?" Four reagent/arrow transformations, each judged separately. `elimination`, not `recall`, and not `application`: the four outcomes are all stated in NCERT.

### `process`
- **Definition:** getting an order, sequence or chain right is the hard part.
- **Tag when:** the student must sequence events/steps/stages, or follow a pathway or cycle in the correct direction; knowing the members is easy, ordering them is not.
- **Don't tag when (vs `elimination`):** no ordering is required — the items just need true/false verdicts.
- **Don't tag when (vs `calculation`):** a multi-step *calculation* is `calculation`. `process` is an ordering of facts, with no arithmetic.
- **Real example:** the PCR-steps question that returned as an arrange-in-order item (`2018#101` → `2026-May#132`). All three PCR steps are familiar; the sequence is what is tested.
- **Note:** `arrange_in_order` (E1) is very often `process` — but not automatically. If the options give four orderings and three are absurd, the real demand may be `recall` of one anchor step. Read the options.

### `concept_discrimination`
- **Definition:** the question explicitly contrasts things that are easy to mix up.
- **Tag when:** the stem or options deliberately place near-identical terms, structures or quantities side by side, and distinguishing them *is* the task.
- **Don't tag when (vs `recall`):** the distractors are merely wrong, not confusable. `concept_discrimination` needs the look-alike to be present and plausible.
- **Don't tag when (vs E5's `look_alike_term`):** these are different tags answering different questions. E4 describes what the **student must do**; E5 describes why **one wrong option** attracts. A `concept_discrimination` question will usually carry `look_alike_term` traps, but a single `look_alike_term` trap on one option of an otherwise straightforward question does **not** make the question `concept_discrimination`.
- **Real example:** menarche vs menopause inside `Q-000015` — statement B is wrong only because the first menstrual cycle is menarche, not menopause. In an item where that single contrast *is* the whole question, the tag is `concept_discrimination`; inside `Q-000015` it is one of four statements, so the primary stays `elimination` and `concept_discrimination` is a defensible secondary.

### `explanatory_reasoning`
- **Definition:** judge whether a stated "because" holds.
- **Tag when:** the question asks whether a reason explains an assertion, or asks for the correct explanation of a stated effect — i.e. the causal link itself is what is being tested.
- **Don't tag when (vs `recall`):** both the assertion and the reason are individually recall, and the options never ask whether R explains A. Then it is `elimination` (two statements judged) or `recall`.
- **Don't tag when (vs `calculation`):** **Assertion–Reason does not imply `explanatory_reasoning`.** A real 2024 Physics A–R item needed a genuine calculation inside the A/R judgement — there the primary is `calculation` and `explanatory_reasoning` is the secondary. This is the single most-confirmed E4 misclassification in the whole sample.
- **Pairing:** E2 `judge_assertion_reason` / `pick_explanation` make `explanatory_reasoning` likely, not certain.

### `diagram_graph`
- **Definition:** a figure, circuit, graph or structure must be read to answer.
- **Tag when:** the information needed exists only in the image, and extracting it is the hard step.
- **Don't tag when:** the figure is present but the answer is reachable from the text alone. (Note that E6's script never calls a figure `decorative` — `figure_role: needed` is not evidence that reading it is hard.)
- **Boundary — figure plus calculation (I am genuinely unsure here; the owner should settle it):** when a figure must be read *and* a value computed, SPEC's tie-break ("primary = whatever makes the question hard") does not resolve itself. **My proposed default:** if the figure is a standard, unambiguous NCERT-style arrangement and the difficulty sits in the algebra, tag `calculation` primary + `diagram_graph` secondary. If the figure's own reading is where students fail — an unusual arrangement, a graph whose axes or intercept must be interpreted — tag `diagram_graph` primary + `calculation` secondary. Say which test you applied in `why`.
- **Real example of that boundary:** `Q-000002` — "the equivalent capacitance of the system shown in the following circuit". Three capacitors in a standard series/parallel arrangement; the reading is routine, the combination arithmetic is the work. Under the proposed default: primary `calculation`, secondary `diagram_graph`. **This is a judgement call, not a confirmed finding** — and it was made from the stem text, not from the circuit. (The figure was absent from the scrape source; as of 2026-10-12 a real one has been recovered to `papers/figures/Q-000002.png` from the legacy-sheet Mathpix source, so this call can and should be re-checked against the actual diagram.)

### `application`
- **Definition:** apply an NCERT rule to a case NCERT does not describe, **without calculating**.
- **Tag when:** the rule is standard, the situation is new, and the step is transferring the rule — not arithmetic.
- **Don't tag when (vs `calculation`):** anything is computed. `calculation` wins the moment a value must be produced.
- **Don't tag when (vs `recall`):** NCERT states this exact case. Check the D4 `link_type`: `states_answer` → not `application`; `concept_applied` → `application` is the natural tag.
- **Real example:** `Q-000003` — a football player moving south turns east at the same speed; what is the direction of the force on him? NCERT gives the rule (force is along the change in velocity); the football scenario is not in NCERT; the answer (north-east) comes from applying the rule, with no numbers computed.
- **Real example from SPEC 12/D4:** the metre bridge with cell and galvanometer interchanged (`2026-May#22`) — `concept_applied`, and `application` in E4.

### `calculation`
- **Definition:** choose a formula and calculate.
- **Tag when:** a value, ratio, expression or proportionality must actually be produced by computation.
- **Don't tag when (vs `recall`):** the number is memorised, not derived. See the 2024 ATP/NADPH case above.
- **Don't tag when (vs `application`):** no computation happens.
- **Real examples:** `Q-000001` (longitudinal stress = W/A — one formula, one substitution); `Q-000004` (ratio of radii of gyration of a solid sphere to a thin hollow sphere → 3 : 5 — two formulae and a ratio); `Q-000009` (null point of the gravitational field of m and 9m, then the potential there → −16Gm/R — two steps, both computed).
- **Biology:** rare but real. PCR 2ⁿ and dihybrid-ratio questions are genuine `calculation` Biology items. Do not refuse the tag on subject grounds.

## 4. Primary and secondary

- **Primary** is the kind of thinking whose failure loses the mark.
- **Secondary** is a second, genuinely different kind of thinking that is also *required*. At most one. `null` is the normal case, not a deficiency.
- Never use the secondary to hedge between two readings of the primary. If you cannot choose the primary, set `confidence: medium` and say in `why` what the other candidate was — the two-run comparison and the adjudicator exist for exactly this.
- Common legitimate pairs seen in real data: `calculation` + `diagram_graph`; `calculation` + `explanatory_reasoning` (A–R with a calculation inside); `elimination` + `concept_discrimination`; `elimination` + `process` (statements about stages, where one statement puts a true event in the wrong stage).
- Do **not** pair `recall` with `elimination`. Those are two points on one scale (how many facts), not two kinds of thinking.

## 5. Two runs, and what agreement means

E4 runs twice and the runs must agree on the **primary** and on **whether a secondary exists** (not on which secondary). Disagreement goes to the adjudicator and raises `E4_ADJUDICATED` (normal). Do not try to guess what the other run will say — write what you actually read. A disagreement that surfaces is useful; a false agreement is not.

If this file is absent, every E4 output is additionally flagged `E4_NO_CODEBOOK` (low) and only the eight one-line definitions are available. That flag existing in the log for old tags is expected; it does not mean those tags are wrong, only that they were made without this guidance.

## 6. The nine candidate categories — do not use them

`trend_ranking`, `classification`, `calculation`, `estimation`, `symbolic_derivation`, `spatial_structural`, `data_interpretation`, `experimental_reasoning`, `multi_concept` were seen in real papers but are **not promoted**. They are not allowed values for E4. Use the closest of the eight and name the candidate in `why` so E0/Stage F can count how often it was wanted:

| If you wanted | Use | And say so in `why` |
|---|---|---|
| `trend_ranking` | `recall` if the trend is stated in NCERT; `application` if it must be extended | "wanted trend_ranking" |
| `classification` | `recall` | "wanted classification" |
| `calculation`, `symbolic_derivation`, `estimation` | `calculation` | name the candidate |
| `spatial_structural` | `diagram_graph` | "wanted spatial_structural" |
| `data_interpretation` | `diagram_graph` if the data is a graph/table; `calculation` if it must be computed | name the candidate |
| `experimental_reasoning` | `application`, or `explanatory_reasoning` if a causal link is judged | "wanted experimental_reasoning" |
| `multi_concept` | the hardest single step, with the second as secondary | "wanted multi_concept" |

## 7. Where this draft is genuinely uncertain

1. **The figure-plus-calculation tie-break (§3, `diagram_graph`).** Proposed, not confirmed. No real paper in the sample was analysed specifically for this split, and the one real candidate in the bank (`Q-000002`) has no usable figure.
2. **`concept_discrimination` as primary vs secondary.** The boundary between "the question contrasts similar things" and "one option happens to be a look-alike" is a matter of degree. The rule above (the contrast *is* the task → primary) is my reading of the one-line definition, not a validated finding.
3. **`application` vs `calculation` for symbolic answers.** `Q-000009`'s options are closed-form expressions in G, m, R. Computation genuinely happens, so I call it `calculation`. An owner who reads `calculation` as "arithmetic with actual numbers" would call it `application` and would also want E2's `calculate_value`/`derive_expression` boundary re-read the same way (see `codebook/E2.md` §4). The two decisions should be made together.
4. **No real E4 run exists yet.** Stage E has never been dispatched against real data — E1's script half and E6's script half exist, and no E4 agent call has ever been made. Every example above is reasoned from a real record or a real paper finding, but none is a verified E4 output. The first real batch should be reviewed against this file before volume.
