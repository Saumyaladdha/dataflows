## 1. What E3 is asking

**What kind of NCERT material is this question built on?**

Not how the question looks. Not what the student picks. The subject of this tag is the **NCERT passage behind the answer**, read through D4's evidence lines and their `kind` (paragraph, table, figure, equation, box).

The single most common E3 error is tagging the question's own layout. A match-the-lists question is not `table` because it has two columns; it is `table` only if the NCERT material behind it is a table. A `figure_to_read` question is not `diagram` unless the NCERT material is a diagram — a circuit question is usually `formula` or `formula_or_law`, because the NCERT content it rests on is a law.

## 2. Start from the evidence, in this order

1. Read the D4 evidence lines **and their passage kinds**. The kind is the strongest single signal:
   - kind `table` → usually `table`
   - kind `equation` → `formula`, or `formula_or_law` if NCERT derives it rather than just stating it
   - kind `figure` → `diagram` or `diagram`
   - kind `box` → often `practical_procedure`, `formula_or_law` or `example_list` (NCERT's boxes are typically worked examples, applications or asides)
   - kind `paragraph` → everything else; decide from the content
2. Read what the evidence line actually *says*: a definition, a value, a sequence, a contrast, an exception.
3. Only then look at the question, and only to settle which of two candidate shapes the answer really uses.
4. Write `why` naming the evidence: "Evidence is the ordered stages of mitosis" is checkable. "Tests process understanding" is not.

## 3. The values

### Plain text material

**`one_line`** — one NCERT sentence states the fact. The commonest Biology shape by far.
- **vs `definition_term`:** `definition_term` is specifically a *term being defined* ("X is called Y"). A plain factual sentence that is not a definition is `one_line`.
- **vs `value_or_data`:** if the fact *is* a number, size or percentage, use `value_or_data`.

**`definition_term`** — NCERT defines a term.
- vs `one_line`: look for the definitional form — "is called", "is known as", "is defined as".

**`list`** — NCERT gives a list of items (functions, features, characters).
- **vs `example_list`:** `list` is a complete or near-complete enumeration that is itself the content ("the four chambers of the heart"). `example_list` is NCERT giving *examples* of a category ("e.g. wheat, rice, maize"). Questions that count or match members of an open-ended "e.g." set are `example_list`.
- **vs `classification_hierarchy`:** a flat list versus a nested taxonomy.

**`example_list`** — NCERT's examples of a category (§ above).

**`table`** — the NCERT material is a table.
- vs `comparison`: a table laid out as rows/columns in the book is `table`; a contrast made in prose is `comparison`.

**`comparison`** — NCERT contrasts two or more things.
- **vs `trend`:** `comparison` is "A differs from B". `trend` is a *direction across a series* (down a group, across a period, with increasing chain length).
- **vs `exception`:** an exception is a single case that breaks a stated rule, not a two-way contrast.

**`exception`** — NCERT states a rule and names what breaks it.
- This is the shape behind most `ignores_exception` traps (see `codebook/E5.md`).

**`classification_hierarchy`** — a nested taxonomy: kingdom → phylum → class, or a classification of compounds/tissues.

**`trend`** — a periodic or structural trend (§ above). The usual shape behind `rank_by_property` questions in inorganic Chemistry.

### Figures

**`diagram`** — an NCERT figure without labels to identify, used for its content.

**`diagram`** — an NCERT figure whose **labels** are the content (A, B, C, D on a flower, a cell, a nuclear envelope).
- vs `diagram`: are the labels what the question depends on? Yes → `diagram`.
- **Real material exists for this:** the legacy-sheet figure audit (2026-10-12) found a real labelled biology diagram (nuclear envelope/ER, labels A–D) for a real question. This shape is genuine, just not yet reachable in the pipeline.

**`graph`** — an NCERT graph.

### Quantitative

**`formula`** — NCERT states a formula or law.
- **vs `formula_or_law`:** SPEC is explicit — `formula_or_law` is a result NCERT **derives**, not just states. If NCERT writes the expression down and uses it, it is `formula`. If NCERT walks through the derivation and the question rests on the derived end-point, it is `formula_or_law`.
- **Example:** R = ρl/A as quoted in SPEC's own D4 example is `formula`.

**`formula_or_law`** — a result NCERT derives (§ above).
- Expect this to be common in Physics: ~40% of Physics questions use one rule plus maths and ~30% chain two or more (SPEC 2.4), and the chained ones often rest on a derived intermediate.

**`value_or_data`** — a number, size or percentage stated in NCERT.
- **Real example with a real change-log hook:** NCERT's age of the universe, "almost 20 billion years" in the older edition → "almost 13.8 billion years" in the latest (confirmed on both real books, 2026-10-09). Any question resting on that number is `value_or_data`, and an option carrying the old figure is E5 `old_ncert_fact`.
- **Caution:** not every stale-sounding figure changed. The biosphere-reserve and hotspot counts are identical in both editions despite being real-world outdated. Only A7's actual diff decides.

### Sequences

**`process`** — ordered stages or steps (mitosis, PCR, transcription).
- **vs `process`:** a cycle closes on itself or is a metabolic/physiological pathway (Krebs, Calvin, the nitrogen cycle, the cardiac cycle). A linear sequence of steps is `process`.
- **vs `life_cycle`:** an organism's generational cycle (a fern, Plasmodium, an angiosperm) is `life_cycle`.

**`process`** — a metabolic or physiological cycle (§ above).

**`life_cycle`** — an organism's life cycle (§ above).

### Chemistry

**`reaction_mechanism`** — NCERT's mechanism or reaction behaviour: how and why a transformation goes.
- **Real example:** the material behind `Q-000005` (which reagent routes give a primary amine — Hofmann bromamide with Br₂/KOH, nitrile reduction with LiAlH₄, isocyanide reduction giving a secondary amine). The content is reaction behaviour, so `reaction_mechanism`. `Q-000013` (Clemmensen-type reduction with Zn-Hg/conc. HCl) is the same shape.
- vs `structure_chemical`: a mechanism/transformation versus a static structure.

**`structure_chemical`** — the structure of a compound as NCERT draws or describes it.

**`nomenclature_rule`** — an IUPAC naming rule.

### Practical

**`practical_procedure`** — a named classic experiment NCERT describes (Griffith, Meselson–Stahl, Hershey–Chase, Millikan).
- **vs `practical_procedure`:** `practical_procedure` is a landmark experiment in the textbook narrative. `practical_procedure` is a **lab-manual procedure** the student performs (salt analysis, metre bridge, Vernier callipers).
- **Hard dependency:** `practical_procedure` should normally co-occur with `syllabus_status: lab_manual` from D6. **The real Laboratory Manual books are not in `inputs/` yet** (confirmed missing twice; the four `NCERT_Manual/` PDFs are model-kit booklets, not the manuals). So a question that genuinely is `practical_procedure` will currently have no lab-manual evidence at all. Tag the shape honestly from the question and note the missing source in `why`.

**`practical_procedure`** — a lab-manual procedure (§ above). SPEC expects the 2026 May salt-analysis questions here.

### Context

**`formula_or_law`** — an application NCERT itself describes (a heater, a transformer, biogas, tissue culture).
- **vs `scientist_history`:** NCERT describes it → `formula_or_law`. Nothing in any edition describes it → `scientist_history`.
- **vs E6's `real_world` stem_context:** different tags. E6 describes how the *stem is framed*; E3 describes whether *NCERT* carries the application. A real-world-framed question resting on a bare formula is E6 `real_world` + E3 `formula`.

**`scientist_history`** — a person, discovery or date as NCERT tells it.
- **Real example:** `2017#111` (Humboldt described for the first time…). Note E2 calls this `name_or_identify`, not `pick_scientist_or_date` — the two tags genuinely diverge here, and that is correct.

**`process`** — a biological equation (photosynthesis, respiration, a population-growth equation).
- vs `formula`: use this for biology's own equations; `formula` for physics/chemistry laws.

**`disease_cause`** — the causative agent/vector/symptom set of a disease.
- vs `one_line`: use the specific value — disease questions are frequent enough in NEET Biology to be worth counting separately.

**`scientist_history`** — a time-bound fact that is in **no** edition.
- **Always outside NCERT.** Real example: the venue of the 2012 UN climate conference (`2015#180`) — SPEC says this is `outside_ncert`, **never** `deleted_content`.
- **Cross-check:** if you tag `scientist_history` and D6 says `latest_ncert`, one of the two is wrong. Say so in `why`.

**`new_shape`** — nothing fits. One-line description; raises `E_NEW_CATEGORY` (normal).

## 4. When there is no evidence (the common case today)

D4 may return `supports_answer: not_at_all` with `evidence: []`, or the question may be `outside_ncert`, or — most likely right now — **D4 may have had no relevant passages to quote from at all.** Stage A has indexed 2 of 13 Biology chapters and **zero** Physics or Chemistry chapters, so D4 structurally cannot cite supporting NCERT text for any Physics or Chemistry question yet (demonstrated end-to-end on real `Q-000004` and `Q-000015`).

In that case:
- Tag the shape the question's **own content** implies, from NCERT as you know the subject's material to be organised — not from the question's layout.
- Set `confidence: medium` at best. Say in `why` that no evidence was available. Medium confidence raises `E3_MEDIUM` (low), which is the correct, visible outcome.
- Do **not** tag `scientist_history` merely because no evidence was found. Missing evidence is a pipeline gap; `scientist_history` is a claim about the textbook.
- Do **not** invent a passage id or quote. Nothing in E3's output carries a quote, so there is nothing to fabricate — keep it that way.

**Expect this to improve by cascade.** When Stage A indexes more chapters, `passage_cuts`/`subtopic_list`/`passage_link` all re-run E3 for the affected questions (`config/cascade.yaml`). Early E3 tags are provisional by construction.

## 5. Where this draft is genuinely uncertain

1. **The passage-kind → shape mapping in §2** is my extension of the four hints in `e3-shape-tagger.md`. The `box` row especially is inference from how NCERT uses boxes, not from a measured sample.
2. **`practical_procedure` vs `practical_procedure`** cannot be validated until the real Laboratory Manual books exist as inputs.
3. **`disease_cause`, `process`, `nomenclature_rule`** have no worked real example I could verify — they came from the v0.1 category discovery and were not individually re-checked in the 2026-10-09 pass (which reported `content_shape` as holding up overall, without a per-value breakdown).
4. **Whether `one_line` will swamp Biology.** SPEC 2.1 says plain one-line questions were 80–93% of Biology papers 2013–2019 and ~38% of 2026 papers. If real E3 output shows `one_line` above about half of all Biology tags, that is a sign taggers are defaulting rather than reaching for `value_or_data` / `definition_term` / `disease_cause`. Worth a Stage F check on the first real batch.
5. **No real E3 run exists yet.** No E3 agent has ever been dispatched.
