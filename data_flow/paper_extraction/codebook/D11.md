## 1. What D11 is for, and what it is not

D9 casts a **wide** net (inclusive, "if unsure, include"). D10 unions those links into candidate groups mechanically, with no judgement. **D11 is the strict verdict.** Your job is to throw out what D9 over-collected and label what survives.

A word-similarity search over ten real papers produced ~220 candidate pairs, **many of them false** — questions that shared words like "Match List-I" and nothing else. Expect to remove members. Removing a member is a correct outcome, not a failure of the group.

**Three things D11 is not:**
- **Not a booklet-code merge.** The same question in booklet codes E1 and E2 is one question, handled by C1, which merges the records and sets `merged_from`/`merged_into`. A non-canonical record (one with `merged_into` set) must never appear in a D11 group. If you see two members that look like the identical question from the same sitting, that is a C1 failure to report, not an `exact_same` repeat.
- **Not surface similarity.** Shared boilerplate is not a repeat. SPEC: never group by surface similarity alone.
- **Not a topic grouping.** Two questions from the same subtopic are not a repeat unless they test the **same fact**.

---

## 2. The mechanics you must satisfy

- **Every input member appears exactly once** — in a group, in `related`, or in `removed`. Nothing may be dropped silently and nothing may appear twice.
- **A group needs at least 2 members.** A lone survivor goes to `related` or `removed`.
- **`first` is the earliest member** — earliest year, and within a year the earliest sitting.
- **Every label is relative to that first member**, never to the member before it. In a group of three, the 2019 member and the 2024 member are both labelled against the 2015 member, independently. Do not chain the comparison.
- **If a group holds two different facts, split it into two groups.** That is explicitly your job, not an error.
- D11 runs **twice** and the runs must agree; disagreement goes to the adjudicator and raises `D11_ADJUDICATED` (normal).
- D11 writes `repeat_group_id`, `repeat_label` and `related_ids` onto each record.

## 3. The labels

Decide in this order: **same fact? → same numbers? → same structure? → same words?**

### `first`
The earliest member of the group. Mechanical, not a judgement (§2).

### `exact_same`
The same question asking the same fact in effectively the same words.
- **Tag when:** a diff of the two stems shows only trivial differences — punctuation, option order, a rephrased instruction line, formatting.
- **vs `reworded`:** has the *substance of the wording* changed? Trivial → `exact_same`. Genuinely rewritten → `reworded`.
- **Real example:** `2013#136` → `2016-P2#108`.
- **Real example:** work needed to raise a mass to height R, `2019#44` → `2026-May#10` — an exact repeat **seven years apart**. Long gaps are normal; do not use the year gap as evidence against a repeat.

### `reworded`
The same fact, in the same question structure, in different words.
- **Tag when:** the fact and the shape are unchanged and the sentences are rewritten.
- **vs `same_fact_new_format`:** the discriminator is **E1's `question_type`**. Same type, new words → `reworded`. Different type → `same_fact_new_format`.
- **vs `related`:** the discriminator is the **fact**. Same fact → `reworded`. Same concept, different fact → `related`.
- **Real example:** `2015#125` → `2019#120`.

### `same_fact_new_format`
The same fact, asked through a different question structure.
- **Tag when:** the fact is identical and the E1 type changed — a one-line question returning as a fill-in-the-blank, a statement question returning as arrange-in-order, a plain question returning as match-the-lists.
- **Real example:** post-synaptic receptors, `2017#106` → `2026-May#179` (a one-liner returning as `fill_in_blank`).
- **Real example:** PCR's steps, `2018#101` → `2026-May#132` (a statement question returning as `arrange_in_order`).
- **Why this label matters more than the others:** it is the direct evidence for the shift SPEC 2.1 measured — plain one-line Biology questions fell from 80–93% of papers to ~38%, with their place taken by match-lists, lettered statements, arrange-in-order and fill-in-the-blank. The same facts are being re-asked in new shapes. Counting those correctly is what lets a generated practice test match the format profile of a recent paper, which is the bank's whole purpose.

### `same_problem_new_numbers`
The same numerical problem with different values.
- **Tag when:** the setup and the rule are the same and only the numbers changed.
- **Real example:** LCR power loss, `2016-P1#40` → `2018#28`.
- **Real example (my reading, not SPEC's):** the stretched-wire resistance problem, `2013#15` → `2017#4` ("melted and stretched n times"). SPEC 2.5 lists it as a clear repeat pair without naming its label; I read it as `same_problem_new_numbers` because the rule and setup hold and only the scaling changes. An owner who reads the "melted and stretched" rewrite as a structural change would call it `same_fact_new_format`.
- **The trap to avoid — this is the important one.** D8 writes a numerical's `fact_line` deliberately **without specific numbers** ("Problem: *setup in general terms*. Rule: *formula*"). So two unrelated numericals that use the same formula can arrive with **near-identical fact lines**. Same rule is **not** enough. Ask whether the *setup* is the same problem:
  - same rule, same setup, new values → `same_problem_new_numbers`
  - same rule, **different** setup → `related` (same concept, different fact), **not** a repeat
- **vs `same_fact_new_format`:** use this label for numericals where values changed; use the other for facts re-asked in a new shape. If a numerical changed *both* its numbers and its question type, prefer `same_problem_new_numbers` and say so in the group's `fact_line`.

### `related` (the `same_concept_different_fact` case)
Same concept, **different fact**. Not a repeat. Goes in the `related` list with `related_to` pointing at the group's first member.
- **Real example (SPEC-named):** `2013#7` (n-type semiconductor) vs `2019#33` (p-type) — **`related`, not `reworded`.** Same concept, two different facts. This is the boundary SPEC singles out, and the one most likely to be mislabelled as a repeat.
- **Second real pattern:** graft rejection was asked in 2015, 2017 and 2019. Whether those three form one group or split depends entirely on whether they ask the **same** fact about graft rejection. If two ask about T-cell mediation and one asks about the HLA basis, that is a two-member group plus one `related` member — not a three-member group.

### `removed` (the `different` case)
Not the same concept at all. D9 over-collected. Goes in `removed`.
- **Typical cause:** shared boilerplate ("Match List-I with List-II", "Choose the correct answer from the options given below"), a shared chapter, or a shared common noun.
- Removing is cheap and correct. Do not keep a member in a group to justify the group's existence.

**Note on the value lists:** `same_concept_different_fact` and `different` are **not** `repeat_label` values — `config/allowed_values.yaml`'s `repeat_label` list is `[first, exact_same, reworded, same_fact_new_format, same_problem_new_numbers]`. The two extra outcomes are expressed by *where the member goes* in the output (`related` / `removed`), not by a label. Do not write them into `repeat_label`.

## 4. Worked procedure

For each candidate group:
1. Sort the members by year, then sitting. The earliest is `first`.
2. Write down, for each member, the **one fact** it tests — from its `fact_line`, checked against its stem and answer text. If a member's fact does not match the group's, it is `related` or `removed`.
3. If two distinct facts each have two or more members, **split into two groups**.
4. Label every remaining member against `first`, using §3's order: fact → numbers → structure → words.
5. Put the group's shared fact in the group's `fact_line` — the fact, not the longest member's stem.
6. Check the mechanics in §2 before you write: every member exactly once, `first` really is earliest, every group has ≥ 2 members.

## 5. Where this draft is genuinely uncertain

1. **The `same_problem_new_numbers` over-grouping trap (§3)** is mine, inferred from D8's documented style rule rather than from an observed failure. I believe it is the most likely real source of false repeats in Physics, because D8 strips the numbers that would otherwise distinguish two problems. It has never been seen happen.
2. **The stretched-wire label** (§3) is my reading; SPEC names the pair but not the label.
3. **Three-member and larger groups** have no real worked example. The graft-rejection case (three years) is named in SPEC 2.5 but not labelled there, and I do not know which fact each of the three asks.
4. **`exact_same` across a scrape defect.** Two records of genuinely the same question may differ because one lost its symbols in extraction (real `Q-000007` reads `"[unreadable glyph]"` where the typeset source has `∮ E⃗ · dS⃗`). Judge on the fact, not on the character-level text, and note the defect. No rule in SPEC covers this and it will certainly occur.
5. **Nothing here is tested.** D9/D10/D11 have never run (§STATUS).

## 6. Proposals for the owner

- **No new `repeat_label` values proposed.** The five plus the `related`/`removed` outcomes cover all 25 real pairs SPEC names.
- **Rule the §5.1 boundary** (same rule, different setup → `related`, not a repeat). It is the one judgement in this file that will shape a large number of Physics groupings once Stage D runs at volume.
- **Consider whether a repeat group may span sittings of the same year.** SPEC 12/D9 scopes the candidate table to questions "from **other years**", so a repeat between two sittings of one year can never be proposed, let alone confirmed here. Real years with two sittings: 2016 (MAIN/PH2), 2019 (MAIN/REEXAM), 2020 (MAIN/REEXAM), 2024 (MAIN/RETEST), 2026 (CANCELLED May / REEXAM June). The 2026 pair is the one to think about — a cancelled paper and its re-exam are the most likely place in the whole corpus for the same fact to be asked twice within one year. I have **no evidence** that such repeats exist in the data; D9's scoping simply makes them unfindable, so the question cannot currently be answered either way. **Reported as a possible spec gap, not a confirmed one.**
