## 1. What E2 is asking

**What must the student pick?** The key test, from SPEC: **look at the options — what kind of thing differs between them?**

E1 says how the question is *built*. E2 says what the answer *is*. They answer different questions and are frequently different in kind: `Q-000015` is `list_of_statements` (E1) and `pick_correct_combination` (E2); `Q-000013` is `reaction_scheme` (E1) and `predict_product` (E2).

Decide E2 from the options first, then check the stem's instruction. If the options are four numbers with units, no amount of "which of the following statements" wording in the stem makes it a statement format.

## 2. The negative-word guard — get this right first

B3 records `negative_words_seen` from NOT, EXCEPT, INCORRECT, FALSE, WRONG, MISMATCH. The script guard then enforces:

| Situation | Required format |
|---|---|
| A negative word is present **and** the options are single items or statements | `pick_correct_statement` **or** `odd_one_out` |
| A negative word is present **and** the options are combinations or match codes | stays `pick_correct_combination` / `pick_match_code` — the negation is recorded in **E6**, not here |
| **No** negative word in the stem | the format must **not** be `pick_correct_statement` |

**`pick_correct_statement` vs `odd_one_out`** — SPEC-named boundary:
- "Select the **incorrect** statement" → `pick_correct_statement`. The question asks which claim is false.
- "Which of the following is **not** a stem modification" (`2016-P1#121`) → `odd_one_out`. Every option is a real thing; one does not belong to the named group.
- Test: is the wrong option *false*, or is it *true but outside the category*? False → `pick_correct_statement`. True-but-outside → `odd_one_out`.

**Real example of the combination case:** a "NOT" question whose options are combinations keeps its combination format. Real `Q-000005` carries `negative_words: ["NOT"]` but its options are four reactions, not combinations — so the guard's first row applies and the format is `pick_correct_statement`.

**Known defect, not yours to fix:** `b3-question-reader.md`'s own allowed set lists only **five** negative words — it omits **MISMATCH** — while this guard checks six (logged in `docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, 2026-10-11). So a real "mismatched" question may arrive with an empty `negative_words_seen`. **Read the stem yourself as well as the field.** If the stem plainly says "mismatch"/"wrongly matched" and the field is empty, tag by the stem and say so in `new_description`.

## 3. The values

Grouped by what differs between the options; the nearest-neighbour test is the part that matters.

### Judging truth of given claims

**`pick_correct_statement`** — the one true option. The default for "choose the correct statement/option" with no negative word.
- vs `name_or_identify`: if the options are *names or terms* and the ask is which term fits, prefer `name_or_identify`. `pick_correct_statement` is for judging claims, not naming things.

**`pick_correct_statement`** — the false option. Requires a negative word (§2).

**`odd_one_out`** — the item not in a group, or without the named property (§2).

**`count_qualifying_items`** — **how many** of the listed items/statements satisfy a condition. The options are numbers like 1/2/3/4 standing for a count.
- **vs `calculate_value` (SPEC-named boundary):** "How many grams of HNO₃ …" (`2013#50`) is **`calculate_value`, not `count_qualifying_items`** — "how many" there is a quantity to compute, not a tally of listed items. Test: are the things being counted *options/statements listed in the stem*? Yes → `count_qualifying_items`. No → `calculate_value`.

**`pick_correct_combination`** — a combination of lettered items ("A and D only", "A, B and C only").
- **Real example:** `Q-000015`.
- vs `count_qualifying_items`: a combination names *which* items; a count says *how many*.

**`pick_match_code`** — an A-ii, B-i, C-iv code.
- vs `pick_correct_combination`: pairings (letter→numeral) versus a selection of letters.

**`judge_two_statements`** — the truth of Statement I and Statement II. Pairs with E1 `statement_i_ii`.

**`judge_assertion_reason`** — A, R, **and whether R explains A**. The third part is what separates this from `judge_two_statements`.

### Ordering

**`pick_order`** — a sequence or ranking. Pairs with E1 `arrange_in_order` and `rank_by_property` alike; E2 does not distinguish time from property (E1 does).

### Numbers and formulae

**`calculate_value`** — a number.
- **Real examples:** `Q-000001` (stress = W/A), `Q-000002` (equivalent capacitance = 2 μF).
- **vs `derive_expression` (SPEC-named boundary):** "…proportional to the cube of temperature … Cp/Cv is" (`2013#22`) is **`calculate_value`, not `derive_expression`** — the proportionality is *given in the stem* and a number is wanted. Test: is the ∝ relation the **answer**, or the **premise**? Answer → `derive_expression`. Premise → whatever the answer actually is.

**`derive_expression`** — a formula in the given symbols.
- **vs `calculate_value` (I am genuinely unsure — see §4):** proposed test — if the answer is a **general relationship** the student must assemble (an expression that would hold for any values), it is `derive_expression`; if the answer is one **specific value** that happens to be written in symbols because the stem gave symbols rather than numbers, it is `calculate_value`. Under that test real `Q-000009` (potential at the null point of m and 9m → −16Gm/R) is `calculate_value`: the 16 is a computed number.

**`pick_ratio`** — a ratio.
- **Real example:** `Q-000004` (3 : 5). Note E6's `answer_form` makes the same call separately: a LaTeX *fraction* is an expression, a `a : b` form is a ratio.

**`derive_expression`** — "∝ …" is the answer (§ above).

**`pick_cause_or_effect`** — what happens, increases, decreases or stays constant when something changes.
- vs `calculate_value`: no number is produced, only a direction or an invariance.
- **Real example (close call):** `Q-000003` — a player turning from south to east; the options are directions ("along north-east"). The answer is a direction, not a magnitude, so `pick_cause_or_effect` fits better than `calculate_value`. `name_or_identify` is defensible if you read "along north-east" as a label. Say which reading you used in `new_description` and set `confidence: medium`.

### Naming and identifying

**`name_or_identify`** — a name or term.
- **vs `name_or_identify` (SPEC-named boundary):** "Humboldt described for the first time:" (`2017#111`) is **`name_or_identify`, not `name_or_identify`** — the *scientist is in the stem*; what must be named is the finding. Test: is the person/date in the **options** (→ `name_or_identify`) or in the **stem** (→ whatever the options actually are)?
- **Real example:** `Q-000011` (name the error type).

**`name_or_identify`** — a person or a date is what the options offer.

**`name_or_identify`** — the group, kingdom, phylum, class or family.
- vs `name_or_identify`: a *taxon or category* is asked for, not a specific name.

**`name_or_identify`** — a full form.

**`pick_cause_or_effect`** — the reason ("because", "due to", "the most appropriate explanation").
- vs `judge_assertion_reason`: there the reason is *given* and judged; here it must be *chosen*.

**`identify_structure_or_compound`** — a compound or a structure.
- vs `predict_product`: if the compound asked for is the product of a reaction shown, it is `predict_product`. `identify_structure_or_compound` is for "which structure is X" with no reaction direction.

### Reactions

**`predict_product`** — the product of a given reaction. **Forward direction only:** reactant + reagent → product.
- **Real example:** `Q-000013` ("Identify product (A) in the following reaction…").

**`predict_product`** (new in v0.2) — the **starting reactant**, given the products and the reagent. Backward direction.
- **vs `predict_product`:** direction is the whole distinction. Confirmed on a real 2022 Chemistry question asking for X given O₃/Zn-H₂O and the two products. Added 2026-10-09 because `predict_product` assumes reactant→product and silently mis-tagged this shape.
- **Build note:** `.claude/agents/e2-format-tagger.md`'s own allowed list still omits `predict_product` (it predates v0.2). The agent is told to use "any added in the allowed list you are given", and `config/allowed_values.yaml` has it — but the prompt is stale. **Reported as a build issue.**

**`predict_product`** — reagents, catalysts, temperature or conditions.
- vs `predict_product`: what is missing from the scheme is the arrow's contents, not the product.

**`pick_correct_statement`** — the name of a reaction or process (Cannizzaro, Hofmann, glycolysis).
- vs `name_or_identify`: use this specifically when the thing named is a reaction or a process.

**`pick_graph_or_label`** — a graph is the answer. Pairs with E1 `graph_options`.
- **Reality check:** unreachable in this corpus today — no option images exist (see `codebook/E1.md` §5.2).

**`new_format`** — nothing fits. Describe it in one line; raises `E_NEW_CATEGORY` (normal).

## 4. Where this draft is genuinely uncertain

1. **`calculate_value` vs `derive_expression` for symbolic answers.** §3's test is my proposal, not a validated finding. It changes a meaningful number of Physics tags (about 80% of Physics answers are numeric or formula-shaped, SPEC 2.4), so it is the one boundary in this file worth the owner's direct attention. **Decide it together with `codebook/E4.md` §7.3**, which has the same question for `numerical` vs `application`.
2. **Direction-valued answers** (`Q-000003`). `pick_cause_or_effect` vs `name_or_identify` is genuinely ambiguous and no SPEC boundary covers it. I chose `pick_cause_or_effect` and said why; an owner ruling either way is easy to apply.
3. **`pick_correct_statement` as a catch-all.** Nothing in SPEC constrains it, so it will absorb anything a tagger cannot place. Worth watching in Stage F: an unexpectedly large `pick_correct_statement` share means taggers are defaulting rather than deciding.
4. **No real E2 run exists yet.** No E2 agent has ever been dispatched. Every example above is reasoned from a real record or a SPEC-named case, not from a verified E2 output.
